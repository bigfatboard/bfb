# Product

## Register

product

## Users

BFB is for small technical teams, independent studios, and hands-on engineering leaders coordinating several coding agents across several real projects. They work in local checkouts, switch between Claude Code, Codex, and Grok, and need to know what is running, what changed, what needs a person, and what can continue without one.

The current operating envelope is roughly three humans, ten projects, five named agent profiles, and multiple enrolled Macs. These users understand software delivery and do not need enterprise process theater translated into friendlier colors.

## Product Purpose

BFB replaces fragmented agent terminals, ticket updates, chat handoffs, and guesswork with one provider-neutral control plane. It launches an agent in the exact approved checkout, reports live state honestly, routes human attention, records time and token provenance, and lets people review immutable evidence.

Success means a team can answer three questions immediately: what is happening, what needs a human, and what proof exists. BFB should make traditional ticket-first coordination feel obsolete without becoming another remote shell or bloated project-management suite.

The home board leads with who is working where, what they are doing, what is behind, and what needs attention. Evidence and the event ledger remain available inside task and run detail, but they do not dominate the daily control surface.

## Brand Personality

Uncompromising, candid, sovereign.

BFB believes traditional software coordination was designed for humans filing tickets, not humans and agents doing work together. It does not beg for adoption or disguise its constraints. The posture is: use it, fork it, or build your own.

The voice may be sharp, irreverent, and occasionally profane when that makes a real point. It must never become adolescent hacker theater, empty aggression, or hostility toward the user. Precision earns the attitude.

Humor is dry and operational. It exposes the absurdity of ticket theater with one accurate observation, then gets out of the way. A useful register is: “The ticket says ‘in progress.’ BFB says Claude needs you.” Jokes never obscure authority, state, risk, or the next action.

## Anti-references

- Jira-style density, configurable workflow theater, and enterprise procurement language.
- Generic purple AI SaaS, glowing brain imagery, floating prompt boxes, decorative glass, and vague claims about “supercharging productivity.”
- Terminal cosplay: green-on-black everything, fake command output, matrix rain, and monospace used as a substitute for a point of view.
- Polite corporate minimalism that hides the product behind whitespace and soft gray cards.
- Autonomous-agent fantasy that confuses process exit with completion or removes human authority without evidence.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the machine working.** Lead with truthful product state, named actors, exact checkouts, attention requests, and evidence, not abstract AI imagery.
2. **Attention over administration.** The first question is what needs a person now, not how every card is categorized.
3. **Earn the defiance.** Strong language and visual force are justified by technical specificity, inspectability, and real operational control.
4. **Human authority stays visible.** Agent activity, human decisions, reviews, and acceptance are distinct states with distinct provenance.
5. **Self-hosting is part of the brand.** BFB is inspectable and forkable by design. “Use it. Fork it. Or build your own.” is a promise, not campaign copy.
6. **Make attention explain itself.** Every request says whether it truly requires this human, why, and whether an agent review can satisfy it. Permission gates are never disguised as delegable work.
7. **Track work, not theater.** Show people and agents doing named work in named projects. Keep the evidence ledger in reach, but do not make audit machinery the product headline.
8. **One dry joke is enough.** Humor should sharpen product truth, not compete with it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for product and marketing surfaces. Status, provenance, and urgency never rely on color alone. Keyboard access, visible focus, semantic structure, readable contrast, reduced-motion alternatives, and zoom/reflow support are required. Motion can be forceful, but no essential state or meaning may depend on animation.
